# Edayar air-assessment evidence checklist

Use this checklist to review records received from KSPCB or another documented official source. Record the source document, page/table, retrieval date, and any limitation beside each completed item. A checked box means the record was reviewed; it does not by itself mean the evidence is sufficient for source attribution.

## Review each facility's records

### Identity and location

- [ ] Match the record to the inventory entry using the registered entity, address, KSPCB consent/application identifier, and documented aliases.
- [ ] Record name changes, uncertain matches, and duplicate or related entities without merging them silently.
- [ ] For coordinates, retain the source, coordinate reference system/datum, units, date, and whether the point represents the facility, a stack, a plot, or only the wider industrial estate.
- [ ] Do not convert a plot number, postal address, estate centroid, or approximate map location into a verified facility coordinate.

### Actual stack-emission evidence

- [ ] The document reports an actual sample or instrument measurement, rather than only a consent limit, standard, design value, fuel record, or estimated emission.
- [ ] Link each result to the facility and stack/measurement point.
- [ ] Capture pollutant, reported value, original units, sampling date/time, duration or averaging period, and any qualifier (including non-detect or below detection limit).
- [ ] Capture sampling and analytical methods, operating conditions if reported, monitoring authority, laboratory, and relevant quality/accreditation information.
- [ ] Preserve report URL/document, page/table, publication or report date, retrieval date, and any caveats.
- [ ] Store actual results separately from consent limits and applicable standards. Never recast a limit as an observation.

### Consent and inspections

- [ ] Record the latest consent status as stated in a dated KSPCB record and retain the order identifier, issue date, validity period, scope, and source.
- [ ] Check for amendments, renewals, expiry, suspension, revocation, replacement, or pending status. Do not assume a historical order is current.
- [ ] Identify air-emission inspection and environmental-monitoring records separately from facility-submitted reports.
- [ ] Keep wastewater, trade-effluent, and other water-quality reports in a separate evidence category; they do not establish stack emissions.

### Ambient air and meteorology

- [ ] For ambient observations, retain pollutant, value, original unit, timestamp/date, averaging period, station identifier/name, station location, source, and quality/completeness flags.
- [ ] Classify daily summaries, historical reports, AQI, estimates, illustrative examples, and missing data distinctly. AQI is not a direct measurement of factory emissions.
- [ ] For meteorology, retain measurement timestamp, location, units, source, quality information, and wind-direction convention if reported.
- [ ] Check whether meteorology is sufficiently time- and location-matched to ambient observations. Same-day summaries alone may not support dispersion analysis.

## Evidence-use decision

- [ ] Facility identity is reliable for each record used.
- [ ] Facility coordinates are verified from an official or otherwise reliable georeferenced source.
- [ ] Actual measured facility emissions are available with pollutant, value, units, sampling date/period, method, authority, and provenance.
- [ ] Ambient observations have usable location, timestamps, averaging periods, units, and quality information.
- [ ] Meteorology is appropriately time- and location-matched for the proposed analysis.
- [ ] Historical, current, measured, estimated, illustrative, and missing records remain distinguishable in storage and analysis.
- [ ] The proposed analysis is validated using suitable data before any performance or accuracy claim.

If essential evidence is missing, ambiguous, or not scientifically suitable, retain the descriptive ambient-air analysis and report: **“Insufficient evidence to reliably identify the pollution source.”** Do not infer causation from proximity, industry type, wind direction alone, consent limits, or AQI. Do not assign facility probabilities or rankings without evidence that supports them.
